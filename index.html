The name

Dar-e-Itr means house of attar.

The name is two words. Dar is the house. Itr is the essence, the distilled oil that the word perfume comes from in Persian and Urdu. The mark above the name shows both: a flask drawn in calligraphy, with a rose and a drop of oil inside.

We keep the range small on purpose. Each scent has a fixed recipe, a note list printed on the box, and a batch number, so a reorder smells like the first bottle.

How an attar is made

Four stages, in order
Stage 1

Distil

Flowers, herbs or baked clay are heated in a copper deg. The vapour travels through a bamboo pipe into a bhapka, a receiver holding sandalwood oil.

Stage 2

Rest

The oil rests in sealed vessels for weeks, sometimes months, until the water settles out and the scent rounds.

Stage 3

Blend

Single attars are combined to a fixed recipe. A mukhallat can carry a dozen of them. Each blend gets a batch number.

Stage 4

Bottle

Filled into glass roll-ons, or diluted for the eau de parfum range and rested again before it is sprayed.
